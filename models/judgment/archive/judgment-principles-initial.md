# Rugby judgment principles — B0

Status: research baseline; owner review pending. B0 is versioned independently of executable model A0. This document can be read and used by a general-purpose generative AI without any repository code, runner, examples, paid service or API key. Local amendments are active only after the owner explicitly approves **B**. Approving A never approves B, and approving B never changes executable A.

## Task and scope

Given an unfamiliar rugby union situation, judge plausible choices, generate a continuation or coaching explanation, then review your own answer against these principles. Use the player's available information, not the narrator's omniscience. The aim is a defensible, conditional account of judgment, not a claim to reproduce a particular human or all laws of rugby. Specify the law edition, competition and variations if known. The safety and legality layer takes precedence over preferences, team tactics and expected gain.

Separate **observed fact**, **remembered observation with age**, **inference**, and **unknown**. Every material assertion about a player or the ball must name its evidence or be expressed as an assumption. Never invent another player's intention, a hidden defender's location, a whistle, possession, or a future trajectory to make a preferred action work. Narrator-visible facts may be reported in a separate spectator section but cannot silently enter player reasoning.

## Priority and conflict resolution

Lower numbers take priority. First remove unsafe or clearly unlawful choices; then assess the reliability and freshness of information; then physical feasibility; then possession, support and space; then individual preference and team plan. A weighted team preference cannot restore an excluded choice. If two principles conflict, name the conflict, state which has priority, retain the uncertainty and choose a reversible or information-seeking option where feasible. In a time-critical situation explain the least-assumption option rather than pretending that more observation is free.

## Output protocol for an unfamiliar situation

1. **Context:** phase, attacking direction, time available, relevant rules/variation and whose perspective you use. Mark missing context.
2. **Evidence ledger:** list observed facts and remembered facts with source/time/confidence where provided. Never invent numeric confidence or timestamps; use “not provided” when absent. List inferences and unknowns separately.
3. **Alternatives:** propose at least two materially different options if feasible; identify disqualifying safety, legality or capability constraints. Give conditions under which each remaining option would be preferred.
4. **Choice or generation:** state a conditional intention, expected benefit, main failure mode and the observation that would change it. For generated action, keep perception, inference, intention and actual physical outcome distinct. Do not narrate unsupported success as fact.
5. **Self-review:** cite the principle IDs applied, report any conflict, check for unseen-world leakage and stale memory, and state what remains uncertain.

When a scene omits possession, phase or legal status, ask for the material missing fact or make a clearly labelled conditional branch. Do not ask for irrelevant detail merely to avoid reasoning. Do not silently assume the executable A model's limited set of actions; B also covers deliberation about contact, kicks and phases that A cannot execute.

## Use without A and review independently

Give this entire document and a new scene description to a general-purpose AI. Ask it to follow the output protocol and review its own answer. Neither TypeScript nor JSON nor the workbench is required. Use unseen geometry, changed possession, changed attacking direction, missing observations and competing tactics. Judge principle compliance rather than whether it repeats an example or matches one action label.

For owner review, score each of these as pass / fail / insufficient evidence: (1) no hidden-world facts; (2) safety and legality outrank gain; (3) memory retains original age; (4) capability and intent/outcome distinguished; (5) meaningful alternatives and conditions; (6) explicit handling of unknowns; (7) self-review identifies material failures. Record the scene, full response, B version and reviewer locally. Do not call an unrun AI evaluation a pass. [Manual review pack](docs/B_REVIEW.md) is optional; all necessary principles are above.

Reviewed 2026-10-11; laws/variations can change. This document paraphrases a small subset and cannot settle every ruling.
- [Baddeley (2012), Working memory: theories, models, and controversies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21961947/), DOI 10.1146/annurev-psych-120710-100422. The multicomponent framework motivates separating working memory from other functions. It does not validate this rugby model, its numeric parameters or its neuroscience.
- Priorities, thresholds, bounded memory, action scores and conservative unknown handling are explicit engineering choices awaiting empirical and owner evaluation. No neuron-level, clinical, elite-athlete or musculoskeletal equivalence is asserted.

## Version and approval policy

B0 is the repository's draft baseline, not an owner-approved final principle system. New feedback is a **candidate**, even when written by Codex. Each amendment must specify condition, criterion, priority, exceptions, unknown handling and evidence. The owner may approve or reject B independently from A, export the resulting standalone document, compare its judgments on fresh scenes, and undo B without changing A. Numerical priority for a local amendment follows the lower-number-first order above; a local amendment cannot silently waive safety or legal constraints. If an amendment conflicts, flag it for owner review. Repository publication and owner scientific approval are distinct decisions.
