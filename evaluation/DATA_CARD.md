# Data card — authored synthetic fixtures and private research records

Status: **provisional synthetic test material, not an athlete or match dataset**. No real names, tracking records, match footage, photographs, voice recordings, scraped play data or private feedback are included in the public fixtures. Identifiers such as `self`, `support-1` and `defender-1` are authored labels, not anonymized real players.

## Generation, labels and coverage

The scene generator uses a uint32 linear-congruential sequence (`1664525 * state + 1013904223`, modulo `2^32`) and fixed 0.2s simulation steps. Units are meters, radians and seconds; +z is attack direction. Geometry and profiles are authored for controlled probes, not sampled from a real population. Body/ability/preference values should not be interpreted as normative athlete values.

There are no human-labelled optimal actions, wins/losses, physiological targets or training/validation/test split for statistical learning. Test assertions encode expected software invariants and known synthetic probes. Public examples and suites are visible to developers, so they are regression material after use. New, independently authored and initially withheld scenes are needed for a stronger generalization claim; changing a name or seed alone is insufficient.

Coverage is deliberately small: one controlled player's observation, simplified pitch/occlusion/hearing, static other players, and incomplete rugby phases. Demographic fairness, accessibility, expertise levels, fatigue, contact, contextual competition differences and real tactical quality have not been evaluated. No real-world frequency, representativeness or uncertainty calibration is claimed.

## Private session records

`.rpm/` contains local snapshots, feedback, candidate revisions, approval receipts, rule versions and comparisons. Those records may contain the owner's own writing and experimental context; they are **not part of the public dataset** and are gitignored. `RPM_SESSION_DIR` can redirect storage; the owner must keep that location out of Git as well. Private records are not automatically uploaded, trained on, included in this manifest or licensed by the public examples.

To reproduce a private experiment locally, preserve its original snapshot/context plus the exact Git commit and runtime, candidate content/revision, rules/profile and active A/B versions. Compare under the same engine; a code upgrade is a separate intervention. Record any manual head/body input, since an arbitrary unsaved interaction history is not a replay tape. Keep the full snapshot private unless a reviewed synthetic replacement is prepared.
